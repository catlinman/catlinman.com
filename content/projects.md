Between 2016 and 2020 I made visualizations, teasers and intro animations for Bossfight, Monstercat, meganeko, nanobii, Sound Remedy, Dex Arson, Chantaro, Jan Dolanský and others, plus a lot of the cover art you can find in the [gallery](/gallery/).

It started with an intro animation Bossfight used as branding for their videos. That grew into the visuals for Elevatia, the Heks EP and its teaser, Nock Em and a run of remixes. Subside with Ephixa came out on Monstercat, and I made the special versions for Bossfight's and Ephixa's own channels. In 2018 I made the teaser animation for Bossfight's Work, and the visuals for Falcon Funk and Bossfight's Comeback, animating the cover art. Monstercat's release of Comeback runs that animation in its background.

For meganeko I animated the album videos for Nascens and Eclipse, and built a visualization suite he kept using for later uploads. One of those is The Cyber Grind from the ULTRAKILL soundtrack, which has passed 22 million views. Bossfight's Nock Em isn't far behind at over 11 million.

Sound Remedy commissioned a fully custom visualization template for his YouTube channel in 2016. For nanobii I made the visualizations for his first self-released EP Love Songs, a teaser for the album Sunshine Express and the video effects for Digital Heart. Chantaro got a complete audio visualization suite for his radio show, [Universe Radio](#chantaro). For Jan Dolanský I made the full animation for the Endless Inside soundtrack, animated and rendered in Unity in two days.

And just for fun, I made a video for zYnthetic's Killing Floor 2 Krampus Christmas soundtrack on my own channel. At 1.3 million views it's still my most viewed upload.

Most of the previews above are from my channel. The full releases live on the artists' channels:

Most of the animation was built in Blender and composited in After Effects, and rendering all the resources took almost an entire week. Episode 37 from June 2017 shows the whole thing in action.

<h1 id="comrelay">Comrelay</h1>
<p class="lede">HTTP command relay in Perl</p>
</header>

Comrelay is a small command line HTTP and HTTPS server written in Perl. You define routes that each map to a command on the server, and a POST request carrying the route's secret runs it. That makes it a handy target for things like GitHub webhooks, and it sat behind a proxy on the Nekodrop servers.

During the start of 2014 I assumed the role of creating a logo for the German band Visual Snow. After seeing what I had done for them they asked me if I could also possibly create an animated opening for their music recording videos that go with each of their songs. Since then I have also taken over some of the editing work for their videos, matching up the video recordings to the music of the commercial song, creating the illusion that they are playing the song live within their videos.

Hivecom is the gaming community I started in 2013 together with Jokler and Trif. It began as Hivemind, a group of gamers and developers with a TeamSpeak server that also hosted the Cobalt IRC community, running on a local machine. Later that year the demand for a better connection and round the clock uptime moved us to a dedicated TeamSpeak server from Fragnet.

After the first Hivemind page I built the PHP site, wired into our TeamSpeak server and into Steam and Discord. I drew the logos and later made a TeamSpeak theme to match. At the time I covered the server and domain costs while Jokler kept the Raspberry Pi running the website online, and the server grew to around 200 registered members, from New Zealand across Europe to North America.

Hivecom is still going, and it looks nothing like it used to. Today's [hivecom.net](https://hivecom.net) is an entirely new Nuxt and Supabase app, built in the open at [hivecom/hivecom.net](https://github.com/hivecom/hivecom.net). The [old PHP site](https://github.com/catlinman/legacy.hivecom.net) is still on GitHub.

</div>

Strikewatch is a top down minimalistic shooter with survival based game play. The game focuses around a timer which will steadily decrease whenever the player is not in a so called zone. If the timer reaches zero, the player is met with their fate. On top of that, the player has to fight their way through the ever increasing waves of enemies while always keeping track of their time and ammunition.

Strikewatch was originally created for the Ludum Dare 27 game jam (2013) under the working title of Timebullet. Since then it has come a long way but it still plays the same as the original game but with a huge set of improvements and additions.

- Fast paced shoot 'em up gameplay with clean visuals.
- Wave based game play with the difficulty increasing over time.
- Time attack based resource management.
- Source code - Modify the game to your own liking.
- Particles!

Strikewatch was written in Lua and utilizes the DaisyMoon engine by [Oxeye Games](http://www.oxeyegames.com/).
